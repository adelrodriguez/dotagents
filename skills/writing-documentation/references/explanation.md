# Write explanation

Explanation is a discursive treatment of a topic that lets the reader reflect on it. The reader is at study, away from the work, and wants to understand. It answers "Can you tell me about…?" It is the only kind of documentation that makes sense to read in the bath.

Explanation takes a higher, wider view than the other types. It weaves the reader's knowledge into a whole, so they work with confidence instead of anxiety. It is less urgent than the other three types but just as important. In many docs it is missing, or scattered in small pieces through the other pages: gather those pieces into explanation pages.

## Bound the topic

The other types have natural limits: what to learn, the task, or the machine. Explanation does not, so set the limit yourself. Use a real or imagined "why" question as the prompt, such as "Why does the client retry instead of failing?" Otherwise draw a reasonable boundary around one area and stay inside it.

Title the page so "About" reads naturally in front: "About user authentication", "About database connection policies". The section does not have to be called Explanation. Discussion, Background, Concepts, and Topics also work.

## Write it

- Give the context: design decisions, history, technical constraints, and the reasons things are the way they are.
- Make connections to related ideas, even outside the topic, when they help the reader understand it.
- Discuss the bigger picture, the choices, and the alternatives.
- Explain how the parts interact, to show why the system behaves as it does.
- Admit opinion and perspective. Weigh alternatives, counter-examples, and other approaches, and give a reasoned judgment when you have one.
- Approach the topic from several directions. Explanation can circle its subject.

## Keep it closely bounded

Explanation tends to absorb instructions and technical description, because the writer wants to cover the topic. Those already have their places. Link to the how-to guide and the reference rather than repeating their steps and facts, so the explanation does its own work and the other pages stay where readers look for them.

## Use the language of explanation

- "The reason for x is that, historically, y…"
- "W is better than z, because…"
- "An x in system y is like a w in system z. However…"
- "Some users prefer w, because z. This can be a good approach, but…"
- "An x interacts with a y as follows:…"

## Check the explanation

- The title works with "About" in front.
- The page answers a clear "why" question or stays inside a stated boundary.
- It gives reasons and trade-offs, not only facts.
- Steps and full option lists are links to other pages.
